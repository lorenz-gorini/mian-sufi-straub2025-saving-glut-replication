### Technical clarification: corporate equity, net worth, saving, and net debt

In Mian–Straub–Sufi, the unveiling system traces **financial claims**, not only debt. The direct ownership matrix includes instruments such as corporate bonds, corporate equities, mutual-fund shares, mortgages, and other claims. The authors explicitly extend the matrix to include **corporate equities**. 

If household (i) ultimately owns corporate equity, that equity is attributed to (i) after intermediaries are unveiled:

[
\text{household } i
\rightarrow
\text{equity claim on corporate sector}.
]

This is an **ownership claim**, not debt owed by the firm's founder to the household. The paper ultimately maps it to the primary asset category “claim on the private corporate sector that funds investment.” 

The three accounting concepts should be kept separate:

[
\boxed{\text{Net worth}}
]

includes all assets, including corporate equity, minus liabilities:

[
W_i=A_i-L_i.
]

Thus higher corporate-equity holdings generally increase net worth.

[
\boxed{\text{Saving}}
]

is the **active accumulation of wealth**, excluding valuation gains:

[
\Theta_{it}
===========

## \Delta W_{it}

\sum_j \pi_t^j W_{i,t-1}^j.
]

Therefore, a rise in the market price of shares increases wealth but is not itself saving. The paper also attributes retained corporate earnings to shareholders as saving through the “corporate veil.” 

Finally,

[
\boxed{\text{Net household-debt position}}
]

is narrower:

[
ND_i
====

## A_i^{\text{household debt}}

D_i^{\text{own household debt}}.
]

Corporate equity does **not** directly enter this object. Buying equity changes portfolio composition/net worth, but not the household-debt position unless the equity purchase is itself financed by new household borrowing.

So, compactly:

[
\boxed{
\text{Corporate equity}\rightarrow\text{net worth}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{active accumulation net of valuation}\rightarrow\text{saving}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{household-debt claims}-\text{own household debt}
\rightarrow\text{net debt position}.
}
]
